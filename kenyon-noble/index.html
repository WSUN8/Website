Our Story Why Local Locations

1882

A Mill at the Mouth of Hyalite

The roots of Kenyon Noble reach back to a lumber mill built where Hyalite Creek spills out of the Gallatin Range, just south of a young Bozeman. Timber from the canyon was hauled to a retail yard on Main Street to build the growing town.

1889

Squire C. Kenyon Arrives

Squire C. Kenyon came over from Anaconda and went into the lumber business. The S.C. Kenyon lumber dealership is where the company counts its beginning, the same year Montana became a state.

1890s

Tom Noble Joins the Yard

Tom Noble, an experienced logger, was hired as a hand in the lumber yard. He worked his way up to vice president, and Kenyon and Noble became partners, supplying early Bozeman with the materials to build it.

1906

Kenyon-Noble Lumber Company

The partners incorporated the business as the Kenyon-Noble Lumber Company. The name has stayed on the door for more than a century since.

1920s

The Carlisle Kenyon Years

Squire's son, Carlisle Kenyon, took control of the company and led it through the Depression and the Second World War, running the business into the 1950s.

1950

The Ogle Family Takes the Keys

After the war, Park High graduate and ROTC officer William V. Ogle came home to the Gallatin Valley. By 1950 the Ogle family owned Kenyon Noble, and they still do.

1958

A New Home on Mendenhall

The store moved into a then state-of-the-art building on East Mendenhall in downtown Bozeman, which would be its home for decades.

1976

The Next Generation

William Ogle's sons, Rick and Bill, took over the company to keep it in the family, growing it from a downtown lumber yard into a full lumber, hardware and building supply operation.

Today

Still Building the Valley

Now on West Oak Street in Bozeman, with stores in Belgrade and Livingston, Kenyon Noble is still family-owned, still local, and still supplying the people who build southwest Montana.

Since 1889

From a canyon sawmill to the whole valley

Kenyon Noble began the way most of Bozeman did: with timber, a few determined people, and a town that needed building. Squire Kenyon and Tom Noble put their names on the company in 1906, and for more than a century the business has supplied the lumber behind Gallatin Valley homes, barns, ranches and storefronts.

Since 1950 it has belonged to one family, the Ogles, across three generations. It's a hometown yard that has stayed independent while the valley around it has grown many times over.
